# Towards a comprehensive gene regulatory network in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*
## Ruihao Li<sup>1</sup>, William K. M. Lai<sup>1,2</sup>, B. Franklin Pugh<sup>1</sup>
<sup>1</sup>Department of Molecular Biology and Genetics, Cornell University, Ithaca, New York, 14853, USA  
<sup>2</sup>Department of Computational Biology, Cornell University, Ithaca, New York, 14853, USA
### Abstract
The collection and synergistic interplay of genes within an organism’s genome form gene regulatory networks (GRNs) that orchestrate gene expression, enabling responses to environmental signaling, cellular differentiation, and the maintenance of stable transcriptional states. However, stable transcriptional profiles across cell types and genetic perturbations reflect underlying dynamical states of the GRN. Inferring GRNs that both integrate molecular regulatory evidence and reproduce these states as stable dynamical outcomes remains a central challenge. Here we present SETIA, a framework that identifies multiple discrete gene expression states from RNA–seq data and then infers GRNs whose explicit dynamical model of gene expression reproduces the observed stable transcriptional profiles across distinct cell types.  
Applying SETIA to bulk RNA–seq data from wild–type and single–TF knockout strains in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, we show that the inferred GRNs accurately reproduce observed transcriptional profiles and can generalize to completely held–out transcriptional states in cross–validation. By incorporating TF–promoter binding and protein–protein colocalization priors at differing strengths, SETIA yields GRNs ranging from highly mechanistic, evidence–grounded architectures to more flexible models that capture indirect regulatory influences needed for predictive generalization. We demonstrate the applicability of SETIA to single–cell RNA–seq data and benchmark its performance against existing GRN inference approaches. Together, SETIA establishes a comprehensive systems–level framework for GRN inference that explains and predicts transcriptional states from GRN dynamics while maintaining optimal fidelity to underlying network structure.
